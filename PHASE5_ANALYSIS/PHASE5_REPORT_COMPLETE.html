4. CURRENT SCIENTIFIC ARCHITECTURE & MATHEMATICAL FORMULATION

This section reconstructs the exact mathematical models currently implemented across SimuPharm's seven numerical engines, evaluating their physical rigor, simplifying assumptions, and failure boundaries.

4.1 High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) Engine

SimuPharm possesses two independent HPLC implementations: the frontend browser engine (hplc_simulator_script.js) and the backend microservice (backend/science/hplc.py).

Van Deemter Plate Height Equation:
$$H = A + \frac{B}{u} + C \cdot u$$
Where in backend: $A = 2 d_p$, $B = 0.05$, $C = 0.01 d_p^2$, $d_p = \text{particle size in cm}$, $u = \text{flow rate proxy}$.
Theoretical plate count: $N = \frac{L}{H}$, Peak standard deviation: $\sigma = \frac{t_R}{\sqrt{N}} \cdot f_{\text{broadening}}$.
Retention Time & Peak Shape Formulation:
$$t_R = t_0 \cdot (1 + k'), \quad t_0 = \frac{L}{10 \cdot F}, \quad k' = k'_{\text{ref}} \cdot \frac{100 - \%\text{Organic}}{40}$$
$$C(t) = \frac{R_f}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{t - t_R}{\sigma}\right)^2\right) \times \begin{cases} 1 & t \le t_R \\ \exp(-0.05(t - t_R)) & t > t_R \end{cases}$$

Hydrodynamic Pressure Coupling (Frontend): In hplc_simulator_script.js, column backpressure is modeled using Darcy's law coupled to an empirical non-linear solvent viscosity curve for binary mixtures:

$$\Delta P = \frac{\eta(T, \phi) \cdot L \cdot u}{K_0 \cdot d_p^2}, \quad \eta(T, \phi) = \eta_{25}(\phi) \cdot \exp\left(-0.02(T - 25)\right)$$
Where $\eta_{25}(\phi)$ captures the well-known viscosity maximum occurring between 20% and 40% acetonitrile/water or methanol/water.

4.2 Ultraviolet-Visible (UV-Vis) Spectrophotometry Engine

The UV-Vis engine (backend/science/uvvis.py) implements Beer-Lambert absorption modeling with multi-component Gaussian peak summation across the standard wavelength range (200–800 nm, 1 nm step resolution):

$$A(\lambda) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} \varepsilon_i \cdot l \cdot c \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda - \lambda_{\max, i}}{\sigma_i}\right)^2\right) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{noise}}^2)$$
Where $\varepsilon_i$ is molar absorptivity (L/(mol·cm)), $l$ is optical pathlength (cm), $c$ is concentration (mol/L), and $\sigma_{\text{noise}} = 0.002$ AU represents typical spectrophotometer photometric noise.

The calibration routine automatically executes ordinary least squares (OLS) linear regression ($A = m \cdot c + b$) and computes the coefficient of determination ($R^2$), slope, and intercept across user-specified concentration series.

4.3 Fourier-Transform Infrared (FTIR) Spectroscopy Engine

The FTIR module (backend/science/ftir.py) incorporates both time-domain optical physics and functional group chemometric interpretation:

Time-Domain Interferogram Synthesis & Centerburst:
$$I(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{P} I_i \cos\left(\frac{2\pi \tilde{\nu}_i t}{4000}\right) \exp(-t \cdot w_i \cdot 0.1) + 2.0 \exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{2(0.001)^2}\right)$$
Wavenumber Spectrum via Real FFT:
$$S(\tilde{\nu}) = |\mathcal{F}\{I(t)\}|, \quad \%T(\tilde{\nu}) = \left(1 - \frac{S(\tilde{\nu})}{S_{\max}}\right) \times 100$$

A built-in diagnostic classifier matches spectral peaks against a 12-class correlation table spanning $400 - 4000 \text{ cm}^{-1}$ (including broad O-H stretch at 3200–3600 $\text{cm}^{-1}$, carbonyl C=O stretch at 1650–1780 $\text{cm}^{-1}$, and aromatic C-H out-of-plane bends at 690–900 $\text{cm}^{-1}$).

4.4 Gas Chromatography (GC) Engine

The GC simulator (backend/science/gc.py) implements temperature-programmed retention dynamics:

Linear Oven Temperature Program:
$$T_{\text{oven}}(t) = \begin{cases} T_{\text{init}} & t < t_{\text{hold, init}} \\ T_{\text{init}} + r_{\text{ramp}}(t - t_{\text{hold, init}}) & t_{\text{hold, init}} \le t < t_{\text{ramp, end}} \\ T_{\text{final}} & t \ge t_{\text{ramp, end}} \end{cases}$$
Boiling-Point Governed Elution:
$$t_{\text{elute}} = t_{\text{hold, init}} + \frac{T_{b} - T_{\text{init}}}{\max(r_{\text{ramp}}, 0.1)}, \quad \sigma_{\text{peak}} = 0.05 + 0.01 t_{\text{elute}}$$

4.5 Mass Spectrometry (MS) Engine

The MS engine (backend/science/mass_spec.py) calculates nominal molecular ion masses ($M^+$), simulates common neutral/radical fragment losses (e.g. $-18$ for $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $-15$ for $\cdot\text{CH}_3$, $-42$ for $\text{CH}_2\text{CO}$ ketene loss), and computes theoretical isotopic distributions:

6. SCIENTIFIC PROBLEM LANDSCAPE ACROSS FOUR DISCIPLINES

To identify where SimuPharm can make a high-impact, peer-reviewed contribution, we invert the conventional inquiry. Instead of asking "What can we publish about SimuPharm?", we survey twenty-four urgent, unsolved scientific problems across four disciplines and identify where SimuPharm's computational architecture provides an unfair advantage.

6.1 Candidate Problems in Analytical Chemistry

  • Problem AC-1: The Inverse Fault Deconvolution Ambiguity: In liquid chromatography, distinct physical faults produce identical visual symptoms (e.g., peak fronting can be caused by column overload, channel formation in packing, or sample solvent incompatibility; peak splitting can result from inlet frit blockage or a void at the column head). An automated, physics-based method for uniquely deconvolving these root causes remains an unsolved industrial need.
  • Problem AC-2: Column-to-Column Selectivity Transferability: Transferring an established isocratic or gradient method between different C18 columns from different manufacturers often fails due to subtle variations in silanol activity, carbon load, and steric selectivity. Predicting transfer failure before running physical solvent is a major challenge in regulated QC.
  • Problem AC-3: Non-Linear Binary Viscosity Effects on Mobile Phase Compressibility: In UHPLC at pressures exceeding 800 bar, the non-linear viscosity peak of water-acetonitrile mixtures produces significant viscous heating and radial temperature gradients, distorting band profiles in ways standard 1D Van Deemter models fail to predict.
  • Problem AC-4: Orthogonal Multi-Instrument False Discovery Rates: Single-instrument identification (e.g., matching a UV peak or single FTIR band) suffers from high false-positive rates when libraries exceed 500 compounds. Rigorously defining the statistical confidence gained by combining orthogonal 1D signals (UV + HPLC $t_R$ + MS fragment + optical rotation) lacks standardized open benchmarks.
  • Problem AC-5: Gradient Retention Prediction from Minimal Isocratic Scouting: Predicting complex multi-step gradient retention times from two rapid isocratic runs often fails for ionizable pharmaceutical molecules whose charge states shift with organic modifier content.
  • Problem AC-6: Photometric Linearity Breakdown in Dense Pharmaceutical Matrices: High-absorbance deviations from Beer-Lambert law caused by polychromatic radiation, stray light, and chemical dimerization lack transparent simulation models for analyst training and method design space boundary setting.

6.2 Candidate Problems in Pharmaceutical Science

  • Problem PS-1: Analytical Quality by Design (AQbD / ICH Q14) Design Space Boundary Robustness: ICH Q14 mandates defining a Method Operable Design Region (MODR) where critical method attributes (resolution $R_s \ge 1.5$, tailing $T \le 1.8$) are guaranteed. Current practice relies on empirical Response Surface Methodology (RSM) that requires dozens of physical runs and fails at the edges of the space.
  • Problem PS-2: Impurity Co-Elution Risk in Stability-Indicating Assays: In forced degradation studies, minor degradation products (0.05% level) often co-elute under the broad tail of the main active pharmaceutical ingredient (API), leading to false-negative impurity quantification.
  • Problem PS-3: Method Transfer Between Analytical Laboratories: Environmental temperature fluctuations (e.g., 20°C lab vs 30°C lab) and dwell volume differences ($V_d$ variation from 0.4 mL to 1.2 mL) cause out-of-specification (OOS) investigations in global pharmaceutical supply chains.
  • Problem PS-4: Process Analytical Technology (PAT) Real-Time Anomaly Detection: In continuous pharmaceutical manufacturing, spectroscopic flow-cells suffer from optical fouling and air bubble transients that trigger false process shutdowns unless real-time software can distinguish instrument faults from chemical process excursions.
  • Problem PS-5: Dissolution Profile Variation Due to Particle Size Distribution (PSD): Relating batch-to-batch $D_{50}$ and span variations to in vitro dissolution kinetics (Noyes-Whitney equation) without running full USP Apparatus II runs.
  • Problem PS-6: Residual Solvent Evaporation Kinetics in Gas Chromatography: Headspace GC method optimization for volatile toxic impurities (ICH Q3C Class 1 and 2 solvents) lacks rapid computational screening for partition coefficient ($K$) determination.

6.3 Candidate Problems in Computational Science

  • Problem CS-1: Global Sensitivity Analysis (GSA) of Coupled Separation PDAEs: Local One-at-a-Time (OAT) sensitivity fails to capture non-linear parameter interactions (e.g., flow rate $\times$ column temperature $\times$ modifier fraction). Sobol variance decomposition has rarely been mapped comprehensively across pharmaceutical analytical simulations.
  • Problem CS-2: Bayesian Parameter Estimation with Quantified Epistemic Uncertainty: Fitting chromatographic retention parameters ($S, k_w$) typically uses deterministic least-squares without generating posterior probability distributions or prediction intervals.
  • Problem CS-3: Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) for Chromatography: Pure deep learning models (e.g., predicting retention times from molecular graphs) generalize poorly across unseen stationary phases. Constraining neural networks with mechanistic transport equations is an active computational frontier.
  • Problem CS-4: Surrogate Modeling of Multi-Dimensional Separation Design Spaces: Replacing computationally expensive finite-difference column transport solvers with high-speed Gaussian Process (GP) or Polynomial Chaos Expansion (PCE) surrogates for real-time web execution.
  • Problem CS-5: Inverse Problem Ill-Posedness in Multi-Component Spectral Unmixing: Resolving overlapping UV-Vis or FTIR spectra without prior baseline knowledge suffers from mathematical non-uniqueness.
  • Problem CS-6: Synthetic Data Generation with Provable Physical Realism: Machine learning algorithms for automated peak detection and integration require vast benchmark datasets with ground-truth peak boundaries, which are impossible to annotate manually from physical runs.

6.4 Candidate Problems in Scientific Software

  • Problem SS-1: Zero-Installation Client-Side Scientific Computing: Most advanced chromatographic tools (CADET, DryLab) require C++ compilation, Python environments, or costly proprietary licenses. Building high-performance, deterministic physics engines directly in WebAssembly / JavaScript democratizes computational analytical chemistry.

10.2 Mathematical Metrics for Model Validation

Many publications rely carelessly on Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) or $R^2$, which are deeply misleading in chromatography: MAPE explodes as $t_R$ approaches void time $t_0$, and $R^2$ artificially approaches 1.0 when compounds with widely separated retention times are plotted together. SimuPharm adopts rigorous, mathematically defensible separation metrics:

Root Mean Squared Error of Retention Time ($RMSE_{t_R}$)

$$RMSE = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} (t_{R, i}^{\text{exp}} - t_{R, i}^{\text{sim}})^2}$$ Penalizes large outlier errors; expressed in native analytical units (minutes). Target for publication: $RMSE < 0.15$ min.

Critical Pair Resolution Error ($\Delta R_{s, \min}$)

$$\Delta R_s = |R_{s, \text{crit}}^{\text{exp}} - R_{s, \text{crit}}^{\text{sim}}|$$ Measures whether the simulator accurately predicts baseline separation ($R_s \ge 1.5$) of the two closest eluting peaks.

Mean Directional Bias (MDB)

$$\text{Bias} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} (t_{R, i}^{\text{sim}} - t_{R, i}^{\text{exp}})$$ Detects systematic dwell volume misestimation or solvent proportioning valve calibration errors. Target: $|\text{Bias}| < 0.05$ min.

Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP)

$$\text{PICP} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \mathbb{I}\left(t_{R, i}^{\text{exp}} \in [t_{R, i}^{\text{lower}}, t_{R, i}^{\text{upper}}]\right)$$ Quantifies uncertainty coverage. For a 95% Bayesian credible interval, target $\text{PICP} \ge 95\%$.

12. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS & UNCERTAINTY QUANTIFICATION (UQ)

Regulated pharmaceutical development (ICH Q14) requires demonstrating that an analytical method is robust against minor, unavoidable operational fluctuations (e.g. pump proportioning drift of $\pm 1\%$, column oven drift of $\pm 1^\circ\text{C}$, particle size batch variation of $\pm 0.2$ µm). Historically, robustness is tested via local, One-Factor-at-a-Time (OFAT) experiments. SimuPharm can pioneer the application of Global Sensitivity Analysis (GSA) via Sobol Variance Decomposition in pharmaceutical separation science.

12.1 Mathematical Framework of Sobol Sensitivity Indices

Let the critical chromatographic response (e.g. critical pair resolution $R_s$) be modeled as a non-linear function of $d$ uncertain input parameters:

$$Y = f(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_d), \quad X_j \sim \mathcal{U}(a_j, b_j)$$
Sobol Functional ANOVA Decomposition:
$$f(X) = f_0 + \sum_{i=1}^d f_i(X_i) + \sum_{i < j} f_{ij}(X_i, X_j) + \dots + f_{1,2,\dots,d}(X_1, \dots, X_d)$$
Total Output Variance:
$$\mathbb{V}(Y) = \sum_{i=1}^d V_i + \sum_{i < j} V_{ij} + \dots + V_{1,\dots,d}$$

From this decomposition, we compute two key dimensionless sensitivity indices for each operational parameter $X_i$:

First-Order Sensitivity Index ($S_i$)

$$S_i = \frac{\mathbb{V}_{X_i}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}_{\sim i}}[Y \mid X_i]\right)}{\mathbb{V}(Y)}$$ Measures the main effect of parameter $X_i$ acting alone on output variance, without interactions.

Total-Effect Sensitivity Index ($S_{Ti}$)

$$S_{Ti} = 1 - \frac{\mathbb{V}_{\mathbf{X}_{\sim i}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{X_i}[Y \mid \mathbf{X}_{\sim i}]\right)}{\mathbb{V}(Y)}$$ Measures the total contribution of $X_i$ including all first-order and higher-order interactions with other parameters.

13. INVERSE MODELLING & CHROMATOGRAPHIC PARAMETER ESTIMATION

Forward simulation calculates chromatograms from known column and solute parameters. Inverse modeling solves the opposite, industrially vital problem: given limited experimental data (e.g., retention times from two fast scouting runs), accurately estimate the unobservable thermodynamic parameters ($\ln k_w, S$) and column physical attributes ($V_0, K_0$) with quantified posterior uncertainty.

13.1 Bayesian MCMC Formulation

Under Bayes' theorem, the posterior probability distribution of the thermodynamic parameters $\mathbf{\theta} = [\ln k_w, S]^T$ conditioned on observed retention times $\mathbf{t}_{R}^{\text{obs}}$ is given by:

$$P(\mathbf{\theta} \mid \mathbf{t}_{R}^{\text{obs}}) = \frac{P(\mathbf{t}_{R}^{\text{obs}} \mid \mathbf{\theta}) \cdot P(\mathbf{\theta})}{P(\mathbf{t}_{R}^{\text{obs}})}$$
Likelihood Function:
$$\ln P(\mathbf{t}_{R}^{\text{obs}} \mid \mathbf{\theta}) = -\frac{M}{2}\ln(2\pi\sigma_{\epsilon}^2) - \frac{1}{2\sigma_{\epsilon}^2} \sum_{j=1}^M \left(t_{R, j}^{\text{obs}} - t_{R, j}^{\text{sim}}(\mathbf{\theta}, \phi_j)\right)^2$$

By implementing Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling (e.g., via the Metropolis-Hastings or affine-invariant ensemble sampler in emcee), SimuPharm produces joint posterior distributions, revealing correlation between $\ln k_w$ and $S$ and establishing rigorous 95% Bayesian credible intervals for predicted retention times under any future gradient program.

13.2 Inverse Inversion of Column Health

Beyond thermodynamic constants, inverse modeling can diagnose column physical degradation. By measuring system pressure drop $\Delta P$ and retention time $t_R$ across three flow rates, SimuPharm inverts the Darcy-Kozeny equation to estimate: (1) Interstitial column porosity $\epsilon_u$, (2) Specific permeability $K_0$, and (3) Effective particle diameter $d_{p, \text{eff}}$.

25. HOSTILE RED-TEAM ANALYSIS OF TOP 5 MODIFICATIONS

In accordance with adversarial scientific discipline, the Phase 5 audit team actively attempted to disprove, invalidate, and destroy the Top 5 smart modifications. A research strategy that cannot survive internal hostility will inevitably suffer catastrophic rejection during peer review. Below are the hostile findings and structural survival assessments.

25.1 Hostile Disproof Attempt on Modification 1 (Sobol Global Sensitivity)

ADVERSARIAL ATTACK: "Global sensitivity analysis via Sobol indices is standard statistical methodology. Applying it to the Van Deemter equation is merely running an existing Python package (SALib) on a 70-year-old formula. Reviewers in *Analytical Chemistry* will reject this as routine computational bookkeeping without new chemical insight. Furthermore, in practical laboratory operations, everyone already knows that organic modifier fraction ($\phi$) dominates retention. Calculating a Sobol index of 0.64 tells chromatographers what they already knew in 1975."
Survival Defense & Methodological Fix

The attack is valid if the paper merely calculates main-effect $S_i$ on a single peak. However, the paper survives by focusing on higher-order non-linear parameter interactions ($S_{Ti} - S_i$) across the critical pair resolution boundary ($R_s = 1.5$) under multi-component gradient conditions. Under ICH Q14, regulatory agencies demand quantitative proof of where a method fails. Showing that temperature and flow rate interactions suddenly become dominant at low organic modifier concentrations provides genuine, publishable pharmaceutical utility. Target journal: Computers & Chemical Engineering, not pure chemistry.

25.2 Hostile Disproof Attempt on Modification 2 (Multi-Instrument Orthogonal QC)

ADVERSARIAL ATTACK: "SimuPharm's compound database contains only 21 compounds. Claiming an 'orthogonal identification benchmark' on 21 compounds is statistically trivial. Any multi-dimensional classifier will easily achieve 100% accuracy on 21 widely separated pharmaceutical molecules. The sample size is far too small for chemometrics journals (*Chemometrics Intell. Lab. Syst.*), which routinely evaluate libraries of thousands of compounds."
Survival Defense & Methodological Fix

The attack is fatal if evaluation is restricted to the 21 clean reference entries. To survive, the study must synthesize 1,000 in silico chemical decoys and structurally related isomers (e.g., positional isomers of paracetamol and ibuprofen degradants) with perturbed spectra and overlapping retention times. The paper must frame its contribution as a mathematical framework for calculating false-positive confidence bounds as a function of analytical dimensionality ($D = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$), using the 21 real APIs as empirical anchors.

25.3 Hostile Disproof Attempt on Modification 3 (SMRT LSS Retention Engine)

ADVERSARIAL ATTACK: "DryLab has been predicting gradient retention from Snyder's LSS model since 1986. Furthermore, dozens of recent machine learning papers (e.g. Domingo-Alaknise 2019) predict retention directly from molecular descriptors using deep neural networks. SimuPharm's 2-parameter LSS implementation is completely unoriginal and 35 years behind state-of-the-art ML."
Survival Defense & Methodological Fix

The attack correctly dismantles any claim of inventing LSS. However, the paper survives by positioning SimuPharm as a physics-informed mechanistic benchmark that exposes the failure modes of pure ML. Pure ML models fail when column dimensions, dwell volumes ($V_d$), or flow rates change. SimuPharm demonstrates that an open-source, client-side mechanistic ODE solver achieves lower prediction error on gradient transfers than multi-million-parameter neural networks, providing an open, reproducible alternative to proprietary software. Target: Journal of Chromatography A.

25.4 Hostile Disproof Attempt on Modification 4 (Bayesian MCMC Parameter Estimation)

ADVERSARIAL ATTACK: "In liquid chromatography, $\ln k_w$ and $S$ suffer from severe parameter correlation (collinearity). Running MCMC on two scouting runs will produce highly elongated, degenerated posterior ellipses. The parameter estimates will be poorly constrained, and the computational cost of MCMC sampling (minutes to hours) is completely unnecessary when ordinary non-linear least squares converges in 15 milliseconds."
Survival Defense & Methodological Fix

The collinearity between $\ln k_w$ and $S$ is real and well-documented. However, revealing this collinearity through posterior probability distributions is precisely the scientific contribution! Deterministic tools (DryLab) report single point estimates, giving analysts false confidence. SimuPharm's Bayesian engine shows how parameter correlation propagates into 95% prediction intervals on future gradients, warning the analyst when two runs are mathematically insufficient to guarantee separation. Target: Analytica Chimica Acta.

25.5 Hostile Disproof Attempt on Modification 5 (Diagnostic Fault Inversion)

ADVERSARIAL ATTACK: "Real instrumental faults do not occur in clean isolation. In an operating laboratory, a column might experience simultaneous aging, partial frit blockage, and mobile phase evaporation. SimuPharm's synthetic fault models are oversimplified heuristics. Testing an inversion classifier on synthetic data generated by the same engine is circular validation. Reviewers will demand 50 physical experiments with real clogged columns and leaking pumps, which will take months."
Survival Defense & Methodological Fix

The circularity attack is lethal if the classifier is trained and tested on identical synthetic distributions. To survive, the paper must: (1) Introduce severe parametric mismatch between the generation engine and inversion solver, (2) Test against historical public Zenodo chromatography troubleshooting repositories, and (3) Frame the work strictly as a proof-of-concept diagnostic framework for PAT, explicitly stating limitations regarding compound faults. This is a State C project requiring careful preparation.

34. SCIENTIFIC & COMPUTATIONAL LIMITATIONS

Honest science demands an explicit declaration of model assumptions and physical boundaries. SimuPharm's current architecture operates under the following known physical and computational limitations:

  • 1D Plug-Flow Approximation: SimuPharm's chromatographic engines treat solute transport as 1-dimensional along the column axial coordinate. Radial velocity profiles, wall friction effects, and radial temperature gradients caused by viscous dissipation at UHPLC pressures (>600 bar) are not modeled.
  • Linear Isotherm Assumption: Peak generation assumes linear adsorption isotherms ($q = K \cdot c$). Non-linear mass-overload phenomena (Langmuirian self-displacement, shark-fin peak shapes at high injection concentrations) are not currently solved.
  • Phenomenological FTIR Centerburst: The FTIR engine's time-domain cosine summation generates a realistic synthetic interferogram, but the final transmittance spectrum is displayed directly from Gaussian band tables to avoid numerical phase correction and apodization artifacts.
  • Simplified GC Thermodynamics: The GC retention engine uses a linear boiling-point approximation rather than true thermodynamic gas-liquid partition coefficients ($\Delta H_{ ext{vap}}, \Delta S_{ ext{vap}}$) or experimental Kovats retention index lookup.
  • Database Scope: The compound library contains 21 curated pharmaceutical compounds. While these 21 possess rich 5-instrument orthogonal data, the library is not yet representative of broad chemical space (e.g. natural products, large biologics, polymers).
  • Decoupled Mobile Phase Chemistry: Buffer pH shifts caused by temperature variation or organic modifier mixing (e.g. ${}_s^s ext{pH}$ shifts) are not dynamically solved by an electrochemical equilibrium solver.

Appendix B: Mathematical Derivation of Van Deemter Minimum Plate Height

The optimal linear mobile phase velocity ($u_{ ext{opt}}$) that minimizes theoretical plate height ($H_{\min}$) is derived by differentiating the Van Deemter equation with respect to velocity $u$:

$$H(u) = A + \frac{B}{u} + C \cdot u, \quad \frac{dH}{du} = -\frac{B}{u^2} + C = 0$$ $$u_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{\frac{B}{C}}, \quad H_{\min} = A + 2\sqrt{B \cdot C}$$
Substituting SimuPharm's backend parameters ($B = 0.05, C = 0.01 d_p^2$ with $d_p = 5\times 10^{-4}$ cm):
$$u_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.05}{0.01 \times (5\times 10^{-4})^2}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.05}{2.5 \times 10^{-9}}} \approx 4,472 \text{ cm/min} = 0.745 \text{ m/s}.$$
