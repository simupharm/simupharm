    ATR Accessory Module

    The ATR (Attenuated Total Reflectance) accessory uses a high-refractive-index crystal to measure samples. IR light enters the crystal, undergoes total internal reflection, and creates an evanescent wave that penetrates into the sample.

    Advantages:

    • Minimal sample preparation required
    • Suitable for solids, liquids, and pastes
    • Non-destructive analysis
    • Quick and reproducible measurements
    Crystal Material Diamond (Type IIa)
    Refractive Index 2.4
    Spectral Range 4000-400 cm⁻¹
    Angle of Incidence 45°
    Number of Reflections 1
    Depth of Penetration ~2 μm @ 1000 cm⁻¹
    Sample Area 2 mm diameter
    Pressure Range 0-80 N

    Cleaning Procedure:

    1. Remove sample residue with soft tissue
    2. Clean crystal with isopropanol or acetone
    3. For stubborn residues, use gentle abrasive
    4. Never use sharp objects on the crystal
    Diamond crystal is durable but can be damaged by strong acids and certain solvents.

    Sample Loading Procedure

    Loading Procedure:

    1
    Prepare the Sample

    Ensure sample is in appropriate form. Solids should be finely ground for best contact.

    2
    Clean the Crystal

    Wipe the ATR crystal with isopropanol to remove any previous sample residue.

    3
    Apply Sample

    Place a small amount of sample directly on the crystal center.

    4
    Apply Pressure

    Lower the pressure arm to ensure good optical contact between sample and crystal.

    5
    Verify Contact

    Check that the pressure indicator shows adequate force (typically 50-70 N).

    Tips for Best Results:

    • Use minimal sample - a thin layer is sufficient
    • Ensure complete crystal coverage
    • For liquids, avoid overfilling
    • Clean between samples to prevent cross-contamination

    IR Source & Optics

    FTIR Operating Principle

    FTIR spectroscopy uses a Michelson interferometer to modulate the IR beam. The interferogram (intensity vs. mirror position) is mathematically converted to a spectrum (intensity vs. frequency) using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT).

    IR Source (Globar)

    Heated silicon carbide element producing broadband mid-IR radiation (2-25 μm)

    Beamsplitter

    KBr substrate with germanium coating, splits beam 50/50

    Interferometer

    Michelson design with He-Ne laser for precise mirror position tracking

    Detector

    DTGS (deuterated triglycine sulfate) pyroelectric detector for standard operation

    Michelson Interferometer

    How FTIR Works

    The interferometer creates an interference pattern by splitting an IR beam into two paths. As the moving mirror scans, the path difference varies, causing constructive and destructive interference. This produces an interferogram which is Fourier transformed to yield the spectrum.

    Help & Tutorials

    Getting Started with FTIR

    Step 1: Collect Background

    Always start by collecting a background spectrum. This measures the instrument response and atmospheric contributions (CO₂, H₂O) that will be subtracted from your sample measurement.

    Step 2: Prepare & Load Sample

    Place your sample on the ATR crystal. For solids, apply pressure to ensure good contact. For liquids, use just a drop to cover the crystal.

    Step 3: Measure Sample

    Click "Measure Sample" to acquire your spectrum. The software will automatically ratio against the background and display the result.

    Step 4: Analyze Results

    Use the analysis tools to identify functional groups, search spectral libraries, and interpret your results.

    1400-1600 cm⁻¹ C=C stretch (aromatics)
    1000-1300 cm⁻¹ C-O stretch (ethers, esters)

    Troubleshooting

    Weak or No Signal

    Check that the sample makes good contact with the ATR crystal. Try increasing the pressure.

    Noisy Spectrum

    Increase the number of scans. Ensure the sample compartment is properly closed.

    Distorted Peaks

    Sample may be too thick. Use less sample or try different sample preparation.
